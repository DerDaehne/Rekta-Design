Straight lines, live tiles, sideways motion.

no cards · two independent themes · four status fills · every value a token · no framework

Rekta Design is a platform-neutral design language; the name comes from Latin recta, the straight line. This site is built in it, and every part of it is a live example: you are inside a panorama, the pivot above names its sections in a fixed order, and the app bar below holds the global controls.

Inspired by Metro.

Rekta Design is inspired by Microsoft's Metro design language, to us one of the best UX designs ever shipped, from Zune and Windows Phone 7 (2010) to Windows 8 (2012). We find it a pity that no major product uses it today, so Rekta takes up its ideas in its own form: its own tokens, typography and components.

  • 2000sMedia Center, Zune
  • 2010Windows Phone 7
  • 2012Windows 8
  • todayRekta Design
  • content over chromeThe information is the interface. Nothing gets a box just to look tidy.
  • authentically digitalNo imitation leather or paper: type, colour and motion that belong to a screen.
  • type as structureRooted in the Swiss International Typographic Style and the wayfinding signage of public transport: large, plain, readable at a glance.

Its token values are derived from the source code of the Porsche Design System (Apache-2.0): numbers and colours only, no code, fonts or marks.

Nothing is decoration.

  1. content over chromeNo cards, no boxed panels. Information sits on the ground, grouped by hairlines and whitespace.
  2. motion as guidanceEvery transition answers a question: where am I, which facet, what just happened.
  3. information-dense live tilesA tile's size follows importance; its fill is its status. It opens something or only informs.
  4. panorama and pivotOne place, wider than the screen, moved through sideways; its facets in a fixed order.
  5. refined precisionHairlines, tabular figures, calm large areas, vivid small accents.

Status is a fill, not an accent.

Neutral ground, no brand hue. Four status colours carry meaning and only meaning: large fills use the dampened tile form, small marks the saturated full form. Primary text sits on every fill and must clear AA; the ratios below are measured live, in the theme you are looking at.

Tiles, not cards.

A tile's whole area is one fill, and the fill is its status. Size follows importance; content follows a few patterns: a stat, a sparkline, progress, the status word. A tile opens something, or it only informs: it never changes data. Press one and it nudges back where you press it. These tiles measure this page, live.

One place, wider than the screen.

A panorama is one place laid out sideways. One wheel gesture moves one thing: started over a section that can still scroll, it scrolls the section; otherwise it moves the panorama sideways. Horizontal input is left alone; at either end the wheel falls through.

Rows on hairlines, chosen the Metro way.

A click selects a row and shows it. Ctrl+click adds another and starts the selection mode: then every click adds or removes, and the app bar carries the selection. Shift+click takes a range, Esc ends it. A selected row nudges, takes the accent and a checkmark.

An event leaves, a state stays.

Something that just happened is a toast: ink, one at a time, with an undo window you can hold by pointing at it. A lasting state of the whole system takes the banner on top: one at a time, the most severe first, its end reported as a toast.

    Checked, not assumed.

    • keyboard firstEvery pointer action has a key; focus moves to what opens and comes back to what opened it.
    • focus on fillsOn a tile the ring is drawn inset in the tile's text colour, so it survives every fill. Tab into the tiles below.
    • colour is never aloneEvery status has its word, every chart a text equivalent, the app bar's square its word beside it.
    • contrast is measuredThe build fails when a pair drops below its minimum, or when two statuses come too close under colour blindness.
    • touch{{touch-target|px}} targets, no hover-only actions, 16px fields, and dragging never activates.
    • forced coloursFills drop; tiles keep an edge, marks and meters keep their shape.

    Same numbers, different plumbing.

    The tokens are platform-neutral; a native app reads rekta.tokens.json: the same values resolved, in logical px, ms, hex and four numbers per curve. Names are the same everywhere: space-m is --rk-space-m on the web and SpaceM in your toolkit.

    conceptwebnative desktop
    unitsrem for sizes that follow the text size; fluid type as clamp() against the placelogical pixels scaled with the OS text size; the same min, max and share of the pane's width
    window-size classcontainer queries on the place, and the viewport heightthe pane's width and the window's height, same thresholds
    densitythe pointer: fine without touch is compactthe input devices present; the same two densities
    wheelone gesture, one scrollerthe native wheel event, same rule; trackpad pans left alone
    backhistory: links, Esc, the browserthe shell's back command, the mouse back button, Alt+←
    reduced motionprefers-reduced-motion or data-motionthe OS setting or the same in-app switch
    themeprefers-color-scheme or data-themethe OS appearance or an in-app switch; two independent token sets

    Tokens first, then CSS and script.

    1. take the tokensrekta.tokens.json for any platform; rekta.tokens.css on the web.
    2. add the componentsrekta.css, with rekta.fonts.css and the Barlow files.
    3. add the behaviourrekta.js as a module, or rekta.global.js as a classic script. No framework needed, and any framework can use it.
    <link rel="stylesheet" href="rekta.tokens.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="rekta.fonts.css">

    { pivot: document.querySelector('.rk-pivot') });
